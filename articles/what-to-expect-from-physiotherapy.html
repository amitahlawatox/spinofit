What to expect from a physiotherapy appointment

If you have never seen a physiotherapist before, it is normal to have questions. Knowing the general steps can help you prepare and make the most of the conversation.

Physiotherapy appointments are not identical. The assessment and recommendations depend on your concern, health history, goals and the professional judgement of the treating physiotherapist. Here is a general outline of what you might discuss at an appointment.

Before the appointment

Think about what led you to book, when you first noticed the issue, whether anything changes it and how it affects your day. If you have relevant referral letters, investigation reports or a current medication list, ask the clinic whether these would be useful to bring. You do not need to prepare a perfect account; your practitioner can ask follow-up questions.

It can also help to wear comfortable clothing that allows you to move as needed. If you have access requirements or questions about fees, appointment length or location, contact the clinic beforehand.

During the conversation

Your physiotherapist may ask about your symptoms, general health, work and activities, previous injuries or treatment, and what you hope to achieve. Be open about what concerns you and which activities matter most. You can ask why a question is relevant or request a clearer explanation at any point.

Assessment and explanation

Depending on the reason for your visit, an assessment may include observing relevant movements, checking mobility or strength, and other appropriate tests. Your practitioner should explain what they propose to do and obtain your agreement. You can ask to pause or decline a part of the assessment.

An assessment is one part of understanding a concern, not a guarantee of a single explanation or outcome. Your physiotherapist may discuss possible contributing factors, areas of uncertainty and whether another health professional or investigation should be considered.

Discussing next steps

Depending on your needs, the conversation may cover possible treatment options, education, activity changes, exercises or a plan to review progress. Ask about the reasoning, expected effort, potential risks or limitations, alternatives and what to do if symptoms change. Recommendations should be individualised rather than copied from a general website.

Questions you can ask

  • What do you think we need to understand first?
  • What are the options, and what are their benefits and limitations?
  • What should I do if my symptoms change?
  • How will we review whether the plan is helping?
  • What are the costs and likely follow-up arrangements?
You are part of the decision. Shared decision-making means you can ask questions, discuss what matters to you and make informed choices together with your practitioner.

When not to wait for a routine appointment

If you experience severe or sudden symptoms, a significant injury or another urgent health concern, seek appropriate medical attention. A website article cannot determine whether a symptom is safe to manage at home.

For people searching for physiotherapy in Brisbane, check the practitioner’s registration, scope, location, fees and appointment availability directly before booking.

This guide is for general education only and does not replace individual medical advice or assessment. Content should be reviewed by the treating physiotherapist before publication.

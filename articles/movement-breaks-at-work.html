Movement breaks at work: a practical starting point

Workdays can make it easy to stay in one position for long stretches. Small, comfortable changes in movement and position can be a useful starting point for building a more varied routine.

Whether you work in an office, from home or in a role that involves driving or standing, it is common for a workday to shape how much you move. There is no single perfect posture that everyone needs to hold all day. For many people, a useful goal is to vary positions and find opportunities to move in ways that feel comfortable.

Why change position during the day?

Staying in the same position for a long time can feel tiring or uncomfortable for some people. A brief change of task, a short walk or a gentle movement break can interrupt long periods of stillness. The ideal frequency and type of break depends on your role, environment and individual circumstances.

Try building movement into existing habits

  • Stand up or change position between tasks when practical.
  • Walk while taking a suitable phone call, if your work permits.
  • Use a natural transition, such as finishing a meeting, as a cue to move.
  • Adjust your workstation so frequently used items are comfortable to reach.
  • Consider a short walk at lunch or during another suitable break.

Keep it realistic. A routine that fits into a busy day is more likely to be sustainable than a complicated set of reminders. Some workplaces have strict safety requirements, so follow your workplace guidance and do not compromise safe working practices.

Make your workstation work for you

Comfort is individual. You may find it helpful to check whether your screen is easy to see, your chair and desk allow a comfortable working position, and your mouse and keyboard are within a relaxed reach. Workplace ergonomics guidance or an occupational health professional may be able to help with adjustments specific to your setup.

Remember that equipment alone does not need to make you hold one position all day. Varying activities and positions, where practical, can complement a comfortable workstation.

When should you seek advice?

If discomfort is persistent, worsening or affecting your usual activities, consider seeking advice from an appropriate health professional. A physiotherapist can help assess your circumstances and discuss options relevant to your goals. Sudden or severe symptoms, significant injury or symptoms that feel urgent need appropriate medical attention.

A simple first step: pick one reliable cue in your workday and use it to prompt a comfortable change of position. Start small, adapt to your needs and follow your workplace safety requirements.

Keep it personal

Movement advice should reflect the individual. Work demands, health conditions, pain and access to breaks can all affect what is practical. This article is general information, not a personalised exercise prescription or a substitute for assessment.
